01 Media Supply Chain

Building a content delivery system from 0 → 1

As a new studio and streaming service, Wonder Project needed a way to ingest, process, and distribute a niche catalog of licensed and original content. I led a lean team of Solutions Architects and Full-Stack Engineers to create a purpose built content delivery pipeline from 0 to 1, then extended it for third-party distribution through our Amazon partnership.

The situation

In 2023, one of Wonder Project’s business objectives was to launch a streaming service with licensed and original content. As a new studio, we had no systems supporting intake, processing, and delivery.

What I had to figure out

As the Product Manager of Content Ingestion and Delivery, I had to figure out a few things:

  • How to build the most lean system capable of meeting our needs
  • System requirements and what we would build vs buy
  • Metadata schema for describing content
  • Delivery spec that studios conform assets to

All under typical startup constraints:

  • Small budget
  • No customers to interview
  • Lean team
  • Accelerated time-to-market

What we built

We built a simple content ingestion and delivery workflow that leveraged a vendor’s suite of content transformation and management capabilities while keeping orchestration in-house. Studios dropped media packages in S3 via Aspera, our CMS could validate and ingest packages, and Brightcove serving as the final destination for transcoded renditions and distribution-ready titles.

Decisions I made

Build vs buy

To accelerate time-to-market without sacrificing reliability, we used Aspera for transfers and Brightcove for video management. Rather than build costly infrastructure for transcoding, DRM, CDN, and content management, we kept validation and orchestration in-house and outsourced processing and delivery to vendors.

Metadata schema

I defined a ~75-parameter metadata schema as part of the content manifest, creating a common data contract across studios, Content Operations, internal APIs, and third-party distribution. It standardized identifiers, descriptive metadata, avails, technical specifications, and associated artwork, ensuring content could be consistently represented and exchanged across the supply chain.

Delivery spec

I defined the technical delivery specification covering video, audio, artwork, timed text, and metadata, giving external studios a standardized set of requirements for delivering content into our supply chain.

Iterative development

Rather than over-engineer for requirements we didn’t yet understand, I scoped the initial platform around the minimum capabilities needed to operate at launch. We expanded functionality as business and distribution needs became clearer, eventually extending the pipeline for Amazon distribution with spec conformance, automated MEC/MMC generation, and MediaInfo-based QC.

My approach

Discovery

Assessed the core needs of a content delivery system for a lean team launching 1,500 titles—delivery spec, metadata schema, and supply architecture.

Build vs buy

Evaluated vendor tooling vs custom build. Chose a hybrid: Brightcove for transcoding, storage, and content management; orchestration stayed in-house.

Specification & onboarding

Defined a comprehensive 80+ parameter delivery specification and led technical onboarding to close A/V and timed text gaps with studio partners.

MVP launch

Shipped the MVP, tested with internal teams and studio partners, fixed validation and ingestion bugs, rewrote the delivery spec, and drove adoption by Content Operations.

Iteration for distribution

Extended the supply chain from owned-endpoint delivery to third-party distribution by conforming to Amazon’s spec, automating MEC/MMC generation, and integrating MediaInfo-based QC.

Outcome

Content Operations delivered 1,500 titles for the Prime Video Channels launch. As of May 2026, 5,000 hours of content had been delivered with 99% performance against the Amazon SLA.
