The problem

A new studio needed a scalable ingestion and metadata framework for licensed and original content. Content Operations had to stand up delivery for a niche library launch of 1,500 titles—with a lean team and no existing supply-chain product to lean on.

The constraints

  • Lean Content Operations team planning a catalog launch at meaningful volume
  • Complex media requirements spanning A/V and timed text
  • Studio-partner delivery that needed a clear inbound specification
  • Later: third-party distribution requiring Amazon MEC/MMC conformance
  • Vendor tooling that covered parts of the stack—but not the full orchestration problem

The product question

What should we build ourselves, and what should we buy?

That decision shaped the architecture: buy where a vendor already solved commodity media work; keep orchestration, validation logic, and operational control in-house.

My approach

  1. Discovery

    Assessed the core needs of a content delivery system for a lean team launching 1,500 titles—delivery spec, metadata schema, and supply architecture.

  2. Build vs buy

    Evaluated vendor tooling vs custom build. Chose a hybrid: Brightcove for transcoding, storage, and content management; orchestration stayed in-house.

  3. Specification & onboarding

    Defined a comprehensive 80+ parameter delivery specification and led technical onboarding to close A/V and timed text gaps with studio partners.

  4. MVP launch

    Shipped the MVP, tested with internal teams and studio partners, fixed validation and ingestion bugs, rewrote the delivery spec, and drove adoption by Content Operations.

  5. Iteration for distribution

    Extended the supply chain from owned-endpoint delivery to third-party distribution by conforming to Amazon’s spec, automating MEC/MMC generation, and integrating MediaInfo-based QC.

Key decisions

Build vs buy

Hybrid architecture: Brightcove for commodity media capabilities; in-house orchestration for control and flexibility.

Workflow orchestration

Kept the control plane internal so delivery paths could evolve—from owned endpoints to Amazon—without rewriting the whole stack.

Metadata governance

80+ parameter delivery spec as the contract between studios, ops, and systems—reducing ambiguity in inbound packages.

QC and validation

MediaInfo-backed spec QC and iterative validation fixes so packages failed early instead of at partner handoff.

Outcome

Content Operations delivered 1,500 titles for the Prime Video Channels launch. As of May 2026, 5,000 hours of content had been delivered with 99% performance against the Amazon SLA.
